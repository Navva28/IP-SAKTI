GUIDELINES FOR PROCESSING OF PATENT APPLICATIONS RELATING TO TRADITIONAL
KNOWLEDGE AND BIOLOGICAL MATERIAL

Source / Authority: Intellectual Property India, Government of India.
Document: Guidelines for Processing of Patent Applications relating to
Traditional Knowledge and Biological Material, 2012.

1.  PURPOSE The guidelines provide guidance for processing patent
    applications involving traditional knowledge and biological
    material.

2.  TRADITIONAL KNOWLEDGE Traditional knowledge may include knowledge
    associated with communities and traditional systems of medicine.
    Patent applications involving such knowledge are considered in light
    of existing knowledge and the statutory provisions of the Patents
    Act.

3.  BIOLOGICAL MATERIAL Biological material used in an invention may
    raise requirements concerning identification, source and
    geographical origin.

4.  PRIOR ART Traditional knowledge may constitute relevant prior-art
    information. Section 3(p) is particularly relevant where the claimed
    subject matter is, in effect, traditional knowledge or an
    aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally
    known components.

5.  SOURCE AND GEOGRAPHICAL ORIGIN For relevant biological material,
    patent specification requirements include disclosure concerning
    source and geographical origin where applicable.

6.  SCREENING AND SEARCH Applications involving traditional knowledge or
    biological material may require examination of the biological
    material, source, geographical origin, known traditional uses,
    prior-art literature, traditional-knowledge databases and exact
    claims.

7.  CLAIMS Patentability is assessed against the claimed invention, not
    merely against the general field in which it operates.

8.  AYURVEDA Applications concerning Ayurvedic plants, extracts,
    formulations, active substances or traditional uses may involve
    traditional-knowledge and biological-material considerations. The
    presence of an Ayurvedic ingredient does not automatically determine
    patentability.

9.  BIODIVERSITY LAW Inventions involving biological resources can also
    be subject to biodiversity legislation. Patent law and biodiversity
    law are separate legal frameworks.

10. CURRENT LAW The guidelines should be read together with the current
    Patents Act, Patents Rules, notifications and other applicable law.
