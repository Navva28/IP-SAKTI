INDIAN PATENT LAW — CORE PATENTABILITY PROVISIONS

Source / Authority: Patents Act, 1970; Intellectual Property India,
Government of India.
 source_url= https://ipindia.gov.in/
1.  PATENTS AND INVENTION The Patents Act, 1970 provides the legal
    framework for patents in India. Patentability is assessed under the
    Act, including the requirements concerning invention and the
    exclusions in Section 3.

2.  SECTION 3 — WHAT ARE NOT INVENTIONS Section 3 lists subject matter
    that is not treated as an invention.

Section 3(c): The mere discovery of a scientific principle, abstract
theory, living thing or non-living substance occurring in nature is not
an invention.

Section 3(d): The mere discovery of a new form of a known substance is
excluded when it does not result in enhancement of the known efficacy of
that substance. The section also addresses a new property or new use of
a known substance and use of a known process, machine or apparatus,
subject to the statutory wording. Its explanation addresses salts,
esters, ethers, polymorphs, metabolites, pure form, particle size,
isomers, mixtures of isomers, complexes, combinations and other
derivatives of known substances.

Section 3(e): A substance obtained by a mere admixture resulting only in
aggregation of the properties of its components, or a process for
producing such substance, is excluded.

Section 3(i): Specified medicinal, surgical, curative, prophylactic,
diagnostic, therapeutic and other treatment processes for humans, and
specified similar treatment of animals, are excluded.

Section 3(j): Plants and animals in whole or any part thereof, other
than microorganisms, including seeds, varieties and species, and
essentially biological processes for production or propagation of plants
and animals, are excluded.

Section 3(k): A mathematical or business method, computer programme per
se or algorithms are excluded.

Section 3(p): An invention which in effect is traditional knowledge, or
an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known
component or components, is excluded.

3.  PATENTABILITY Patent examination applies the statutory requirements
    of the Patents Act and Rules, including novelty, inventive step and
    industrial applicability where applicable.

4.  SPECIFICATIONS Section 9 distinguishes provisional and complete
    specifications. Section 10 sets requirements for specifications,
    including description of the invention, its operation or use, the
    method of performance and disclosure of the best method known to the
    applicant for the claimed invention.

Claims define the scope of protection sought.

Where biological material is involved, the Act contains applicable
provisions concerning deposit and disclosure of source and geographical
origin.

5.  PRIORITY Section 11 addresses priority dates and the effective dates
    associated with claims.

6.  EXAMINATION Patent applications are examined by the Patent Office
    according to the Act and Rules.

7.  OPPOSITION Section 25 provides grounds and procedures for pre-grant
    and post-grant opposition. Grounds include specified matters
    involving prior publication, prior claiming, prior knowledge or use,
    obviousness or lack of inventive step, non-patentable subject
    matter, insufficient disclosure, and specified issues concerning
    biological material and traditional knowledge.

8.  REVOCATION Section 64 provides statutory grounds for revocation of
    patents.

9.  PATENT RIGHTS Section 48 sets out rights of a patentee, subject to
    the Act. Product and process patent rights are described in the
    section.

10. AYURVEDA CONTEXT Patent protection applies to patentable inventions
    under the Act. It does not create a general monopoly over
    traditional knowledge, naturally occurring substances or established
    traditional practices.

Source note: This is a factual summary of the official Indian patent
framework. The current statutory text remains authoritative.
