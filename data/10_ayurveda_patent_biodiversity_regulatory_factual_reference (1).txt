1.  AYURVEDA AND INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY Ayurvedic knowledge can intersect
    with patents and other intellectual-property rights. Patent
    protection applies to patentable inventions under the Patents Act,
    1970 and does not automatically protect traditional knowledge or
    naturally occurring substances.

2.  TRADITIONAL KNOWLEDGE Section 3(p) excludes an invention that in
    effect is traditional knowledge or an aggregation or duplication of
    known properties of traditionally known components.

TKDL documents traditional knowledge from Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, Sowa
Rigpa and Yoga.

3.  KNOWN SUBSTANCES Section 3(d) addresses new forms and new uses of
    known substances and excludes the subject matter specified by the
    statutory provision.

4.  ADMIXTURES Section 3(e) addresses substances obtained by mere
    admixture when the result is only aggregation of the properties of
    the components.

5.  MEDICAL TREATMENT Section 3(i) excludes specified medicinal,
    surgical, curative, prophylactic, diagnostic, therapeutic and other
    treatment processes for humans and specified similar treatment of
    animals.

6.  PLANTS AND ANIMALS Section 3(j) excludes plants and animals in whole
    or part, other than microorganisms, subject to the exact statutory
    wording, and addresses essentially biological processes for
    production or propagation.

7.  BIOLOGICAL MATERIAL The Patents Act contains specification
    requirements concerning biological material, including applicable
    deposit provisions and disclosure of source and geographical origin.

8.  BIODIVERSITY The Biological Diversity Act, 2002, as amended,
    regulates specified access to biological resources and associated
    requirements. The 2023 amendment came into force on 1 April 2024.

9.  ASU MEDICINES Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani drugs are regulated under
    the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules, including manufacturing,
    testing, labelling and GMP requirements.

10. AYURVEDA AAHARA Ayurveda Aahara is regulated as a food category
    under the Food Safety and Standards (Ayurveda Aahara)
    Regulations, 2022. The regulations define the category by reference
    to specified Ayurvedic recipes, ingredients and processes and
    contain exclusions distinguishing it from Ayurvedic drugs and other
    products.

11. PRODUCT CLASSIFICATION Product classification depends on statutory
    definitions and facts such as ingredients, intended use, claims,
    dosage or food form and applicable exclusions.

12. INTERNATIONAL PROTECTION The PCT provides an international filing
    route but does not grant a worldwide patent. The usual priority
    period is 12 months, and a common national-phase deadline is 30
    months from the priority date.

13. WIPO GENETIC-RESOURCE TREATY WIPO adopted the Treaty on Intellectual
    Property, Genetic Resources and Associated Traditional Knowledge on
    24 May 2024. It includes disclosure provisions concerning the
    country of origin or source of genetic resources and associated
    traditional knowledge in relevant patent applications.

14. OFFICIAL AUTHORITIES Relevant official authorities include
    Intellectual Property India for Indian patent law, CDSCO for drug
    regulation, FSSAI for food regulation, the National Biodiversity
    Authority and India Code for biodiversity law, WIPO for
    international patent and treaty systems, and TKDL / Government of
    India sources for traditional-knowledge documentation.

15. CURRENT STATUS Laws, regulations, amendments, notifications and

    authoritative.
