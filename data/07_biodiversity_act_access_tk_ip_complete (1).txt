BIOLOGICAL DIVERSITY LAW — ACCESS, TRADITIONAL KNOWLEDGE AND
INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

Source / Authority: Government of India, India Code and National
Biodiversity Authority. Primary framework: Biological Diversity Act,
2002, as amended, with applicable rules and regulations.
 source_url = https://nbaindia.org/
1.  PURPOSE The biodiversity framework regulates specified access to
    biological resources and associated legal requirements and provides
    a framework for conservation, sustainable use and benefit sharing.

2.  BIOLOGICAL RESOURCES The Act contains a statutory definition of
    biological resources and specified exclusions. The current amended
    text is authoritative.

3.  ACCESS Section 3 contains restrictions and approval requirements
    concerning access by specified persons, including persons who are
    not citizens of India and specified non-resident or foreign
    entities. Applicability depends on the statutory facts.

4.  TRANSFER OF RESEARCH RESULTS Section 4 addresses transfer of
    research results relating to biological resources covered by the Act
    to persons or entities described in the provision.

5.  INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS Section 6 addresses applications for
    intellectual property rights based on research or information on
    biological resources obtained from India in circumstances covered by
    the Act. The exact mechanism depends on the current amended law and
    applicable regulations.

6.  INDIAN CITIZENS AND ENTITIES Section 7 and related provisions
    address access by Indian citizens and entities in circumstances
    specified by the Act and amendments.

7.  TRADITIONAL KNOWLEDGE Traditional knowledge associated with
    biological resources can be relevant to biodiversity regulation as
    well as patent law.

8.  BENEFIT SHARING The framework provides for fair and equitable
    benefit sharing according to the Act and applicable rules and
    regulations.

9.  2023 AMENDMENT The Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023
    introduced changes to the biodiversity framework and came into force
    on 1 April 2024.

10. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY DISTINCTION Patent filing and biodiversity
    compliance are separate legal questions. A patent application
    involving biological resources may need to satisfy both patent-law
    and biodiversity-law requirements.

11. AUTHORITIES The National Biodiversity Authority is a statutory
    authority associated with implementation of the biodiversity
    framework. State Biodiversity Boards and Biodiversity Management
    Committees also have roles under the framework.

Source note: This document avoids fixed approval percentages, fees and
