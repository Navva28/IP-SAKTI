AYURVEDIC, SIDDHA AND UNANI DRUGS — REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

Source / Authority: Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO),
Government of India. Primary framework: Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940
and Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945.
source_url = https://cdsco.gov.in/
1.  SCOPE The regulatory framework covers Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani
    (ASU) drugs under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules, together
    with applicable schedules, notifications and amendments.

2.  ASU DRUGS The framework covers medicines used in Ayurvedic, Siddha
    and Unani systems, including classical medicines and proprietary
    medicines as defined by the applicable law.

3.  MANUFACTURE Part XVI of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules contains
    provisions concerning manufacture of ASU drugs, including
    requirements relating to premises, equipment, personnel, records,
    quality control and other applicable matters.

4.  LABELLING AND PACKING Part XVII contains provisions concerning
    labelling and packing of ASU drugs, including provisions relevant to
    alcoholic preparations and other specified products.

5.  ANALYSTS AND INSPECTORS Part XVIII contains provisions concerning
    government analysts and inspectors for ASU drugs.

6.  PATENT OR PROPRIETARY MEDICINE The Drugs and Cosmetics Rules contain
    definitions for Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani patent or proprietary
    medicines. This regulatory concept is separate from patentability
    under patent law.

7.  CLASSICAL MEDICINES Classical medicines are associated with
    formulations described in authoritative books of the relevant
    traditional system, subject to the precise statutory definition.

8.  QUALITY AND SAFETY ASU drug regulation includes requirements
    intended to control medicine quality and safety, including
    applicable manufacturing, testing and labelling requirements.

9.  SCHEDULE T Schedule T contains Good Manufacturing Practices
    requirements for Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani medicines.

10. PATENT LAW DISTINCTION Regulatory classification or approval of an
    ASU medicine does not itself establish patentability. Patentability
    is separately assessed under the Patents Act, 1970.
